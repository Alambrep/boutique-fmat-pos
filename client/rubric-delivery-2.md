# Rubric — Delivery 2

Transcribed and translated from the original checklist (*Hoja de cotejo — Segunda entrega*): [`originals/Rubric_2nd_Delivery-HCI.pdf`](originals/Rubric_2nd_Delivery-HCI.pdf). The Spanish original prevails. For links to every section, see the [rubric map](../RUBRIC-MAP.md).

Scale: Needs much improvement (1) · Minimal (2) · Fair (3) · Good (4) · Excellent (5)

## 1. Evolution of the requirements

| Item | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| 1a — Changes in requirements | Significant changes in the functional and non-functional requirements. The evolution is shown by the clarity of the language used to describe the requirements (FR and NFR). |
| 1b — Relationship between requirements | A clear relationship/mapping between functional and non-functional requirements, particularly those related to usability. |
| 1c — Specification of usability requirements | The NFRs state clearly which usability characteristics/attributes are considered, either at system level or for a particular requirement. |
| 1d — Requirements traceability | Artifacts are developed that map requirements to the design and support traceability. |

## 2. Interface prototyping

| Item | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| 2a — Prototype development | Evidence in the repository of the development/evolution of high-, medium- or low-fidelity prototypes. |
| 2b — Design guidelines | The team adopted or designed design guidelines for the interfaces. They cover at least the organization of interface elements and the information architecture; they may include low- or high-fidelity visual aspects. |
| 2c — Evolution of prototypes | Depending on the team's progress, evidence of how the interface design evolved, including the design team's considerations. This evidence is optional and depends on progress at the time of delivery. |
| 2d — Interaction design | The key interactions of the system (essential functionality or greatest complexity) are identified. How the user performs each process is defined clearly and in a standardized way, based on the functional requirements and the usability non-functional requirements. Interaction design can be used as a traceability tool, particularly for non-functional requirements. |

## 3. Inspection methods (optional)

| Item | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| 3a — Inspection method | An inspection method was used, selected according to its purpose; results are presented, together with how they were used in the interface or interaction design. |
| 3b — Tool or protocol | Evidence of the tool or protocol used to apply the selected method. |

## 4. Usability test sketch

| Item | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| 4a — Selection of usability NFRs | Selected according to the system's priority usability NFRs, based on objective, well-defined aspects. |
| 4b — Test planning | The plan covers: selection and number of participants; selection of tasks and test scenarios based on the usability goals; time estimates per task and for the whole test; instruments, tools and materials (e.g. questionnaires) to collect data about participants and tasks. |
| 4c — Usability metrics | Definition/selection of quantitative and qualitative usability measures, with levels for each metric (unacceptable, acceptable, excellent). |
| 4d — Usability test protocol | An artifact that describes clearly and precisely each of the previous aspects and would allow a pilot test. |

## 5. Teamwork

| Item | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| 5a — Logbook / evidence of the team's process | Evidence in the repository that shows the project's progress and reflects a development process and/or the team's planning. The tracking log shows clearly the tasks/activities, owner and status (Completed, In progress, Cancelled), plus the minimum information to know when a task is considered complete. |
| 5b — Individual contribution metric | A description of the metric and its parameters (e.g. hours, task complexity, number of tasks), based on objective quantitative/qualitative parameters; an artifact to track individual progress; a table of % individual contribution per delivery (100% divided among the team members). |

## 6. Progress presentation

| Item | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| 6a — Presentation material | Slides/material present the information clearly and concisely; clear sequence; balanced participation; repository URL included for details. |
| 6b — Quality of content | The order of the presentation shows the project's progress through the user-centred design stages and the software engineering activities involved; the most relevant information is synthesized. |
| 6c — Presentation time | Within the time limit (5 minutes, as stated by the author); time balanced across the points presented. |
